# Curse of the Azure Bonds — Field Guide

A condensed reference for the game the trainer edits. Everything here is drawn from material that
ships with this install — the Rule Book and Adventure Journal bundled as `curseazure.pdf`, and the
game's own data files — rather than from a walkthrough. Where something is general Gold Box tactics
rather than a statement the game makes, it says so.

The in-app **🗺 Strategy** tab is a shorter version of this.

---

## 1. The setup

Your party came south to Tilverton, on the border between Cormyr and the Dalelands, hunting the
runaway princess **Nacacia of Cormyr** and the reward King Azoun IV had out for her return. Nacacia
had fled an arranged marriage a year earlier with a Tilverton cleric named **Gharri of Gond**; word
was the two had fallen out and she had been seen near the town.

On the road you were ambushed. The journal's own account: attackers in evil-looking helmets, probably
invisible, firing crossbows — *"The crossbow hits were not fatal, but every hit seemed to drop its
target."* You wake in Tilverton roughly a month later. Your wounds are healed, your equipment is
gone, you have a stash of coins, and each character has **five azure-blue symbols imprinted under
the skin of the sword arm**. They are not tattoos. They sometimes feel like they are moving, and each
one can seize control of your actions.

The quest is to find who put them there and get them off. The game's own ending text marks the
finish precisely: *"You are certain he is destroyed because your final bond fades away."*

## 2. Five bonds, five chapters

The game's resource archives are numbered by chapter, and each chapter carries its own monster
roster. Those rosters name the chapters, which is how the Maps tab groups its levels:

| Chapter | Where | Who holds the bond | Levels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | **Tilverton** | the Fire Knives | Streets, the Pit, Sewers |
| 3 | **Yulash and the Temple of Moander** | Mogion, priestess of Moander | Ruined Town, Tunnels, Temple |
| 4 | **Zhentil Keep** | the Zhentarim | Streets, Upper Level, Dungeon |
| 5 | **Dracandros's stronghold** | the mage Dracandros | Grounds, Tower, Vault |
| 6 | **Myth Drannor** | Tyranthraxus the Flamed One | Outer Ruins, Inner Ruins, Catacombs, Sanctum |

Chapter 1 has no level geometry at all — it is the wilderness and its random encounters.

**Alias** and **Dragonbait** have character records in chapter 3, which is where they enter the
story. **Akabar bel Akash** appears in chapter 5.

## 3. The Dalelands, as the journal describes them

- **Cormyr** — the large civilised nation south and west of the Dalelands. It has annexed Tilverton.
- **Tilverton** — a small city on Cormyr's northern border, occupied peacefully by Cormyr's 7th
  Division. Where you wake up.
- **Shadowdale** — a haven for adventurers where the Ashaba crosses the Tilverton–Voonlar road, and
  home of the sage **Elminster**.
- **Yulash** — a ruined city on the only trade route from Zhentil Keep to the southlands, held by the
  Red Plume mercenaries of Hillsfar after a bitter fight with Zhentil Keep. Both sides are preparing
  for another round. Moander's power was centred here.
- **Zhentil Keep** — politically controlled by the evil Zhentarim, expanding west and south.
- **Myth Drannor** — the vast ancient elven city at the centre of the Elven Court, deserted when the
  elves left and overrun by evil creatures drawn to its places of power.
- **The Standing Stone** — the marker commemorating the elves' agreement letting humans settle the
  Dalelands, and the nearest landmark to Myth Drannor.
- **Teshwave**, **Voonlar** — recently occupied by, and allied with, Zhentil Keep.
- **Hillsfar**, **Essembra**, **Mistledale**, **Dagger Falls** — the surrounding dales and towns.

**Power groups.** The Army of Zhentil Keep is a tool of the Zhentarim and fights with close
cooperation between fighters, mages and clerics, plus "Terror Teams" sent behind enemy lines. The
**Cultists of Moander** were central to creating the first azure bonds, and began a fresh plot after
Moander was banished over Westgate; their symbol is a black hand with a mouth in its palm. The
**Fire Knives** are thieves and assassins driven out of Cormyr by King Azoun, sworn to assassinate
him, and joined the first bonding project to that end; their symbol is a dagger wreathed in flame.
The **Harpers** and the **Knights of Myth Drannor** oppose them.

## 4. Starting out

- New characters begin with **25,000 experience points** and the level that buys — 5th for most
  single-class characters. A non-human multi-class character divides all experience between its
  classes, so it starts at 12,500 per class and often a level lower.
- Each character begins with **300 platinum pieces** (= 1,500 gp). You were robbed of everything, so
  spend it on arms and armour before leaving town.
- Characters can be imported from **Pool of Radiance** and from **Hillsfar**. Remove them from their
  old party before transferring.
- Training costs **1,000 gold per level**.
- The **translation wheel** is the copy protection: match the espruar (elvish) and dethek (dwarvish)
  runes the game shows, then read the code letter through the numbered hole under the given path.

## 5. Classes, races and limits

Curse runs to level 12, so racial limits bite harder than in the first game.

| Class | Hit die | Cap (human) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fighter | d10 | 12 | Any armour, any weapon. Only class group with exceptional 18/xx Strength. |
| Cleric | d8 | 10 | Any armour; club, flail, hammer, mace, staff, staff sling. Humans and half-elves only. |
| Paladin | d10 | 11 | Must be Lawful Good. Saves 2 better than a fighter; permanent protection from evil. Human only. |
| Ranger | d8 (2d8 at 1st) | 11 | Extra hit dice; first-level druid and magic-user spells at 8th and 9th. |
| Magic-User | d4 | 11 | No armour; dagger, dart, staff. Human, elf or half-elf. |
| Thief | d6 | 12 | Leather only. **No racial level limit** — every race reaches 12. |

Racial caps worth knowing: a dwarf fighter stops at 9 with Strength 18+, a halfling at 5, an elf
fighter at 7 but an elf magic-user at 11 with Intelligence 18. Half-elves have the widest
multi-class menu, including cleric/fighter/mage. Dwarves, gnomes and halflings cannot cast at all.

Racial abilities show up in the record as permanent effects: dwarves carry a THAC0 bonus, a bonus
against giants and a saving-throw bonus; elves carry 90% resistance to sleep and charm.

## 6. Spells that decide fights

Both spell lists reach **fifth level** in Curse, which is the headline change from the first game.

- **Sleep** (mage 1) — up to sixteen one-hit-die targets, no saving throw; nothing of 5 hit dice or
  more is affected. Ends most early encounters outright.
- **Haste** (mage 3) — doubles movement and melee attacks. The strongest buff in the game.
- **Fireball** / **Lightning Bolt** (mage 3) — 1d6 per caster level. Mind the radius, and mind that a
  bolt *rebounds off walls* to reach its full length, which can bring it back through your party.
- **Prayer** (cleric 3) — your side +1, theirs −1: a two-point swing.
- **Hold Person** — paralyses up to 3 targets for a cleric, 4 for a mage; a held target is trivial to
  finish.
- **Silence 15' Radius** (cleric 2) — the answer to enemy casters.
- **Cure Light/Serious/Critical Wounds** (cleric 1/4/5) — 1–8, 3–17, 6–27 HP.
- **Raise Dead** (cleric 5) — any non-elf character. Elves cannot be raised, ever.
- **Cloudkill** and **Cone of Cold** (mage 5) — the late-game clears.
- **Dispel Magic**, **Remove Curse**, **Neutralize Poison** — keep one of each available.

Clerics with high Wisdom get bonus spells, but only at levels they already have slots for: Wisdom 17
adds +2/+2/+1 to spell levels 1–3.

## 8. Money and weight

Coins weigh, and the character record's encumbrance field counts them — a character carrying nothing
but their starting 300 platinum already registers 300. Convert bulk coin into **gems** and
**jewelry** (the record keeps separate counters) when a jeweller offers. Maxing money in the trainer
will floor a character's movement; trim it if that matters.

Armor as the Rule Book lists it, with the movement each allows:

| Armor | AC | Movement |
|---|---:|---|
| None | 10 | 12 squares |
| Leather | 8 | 12 squares |
| Padded / Studded / Ring | 8 / 7 / 7 | 9 squares |
| Chain | 5 | 9 squares |
| Banded | 4 | 9 squares |
| Scale / Splint | 6 / 4 | 6 squares |
| Plate | 3 | 6 squares |

A shield subtracts a further 1.

## 9. Using the trainer

- **God mode** freezes party hit points each poll tick and holds through a whole battle.
- **🩸 Weaken** is the loot-safe way to win a fight: it leaves the enemy on 1 HP with AC and THAC0
  20, so your next blow lands and kills — and because the *game* applies the killing blow, the body
  drops and you get the treasure and the experience. Zeroing a record instead ends the fight in a
  surrender, which pays nothing.
- **🧪 Restore drained** puts every ability score back to the maximum the record stores for it. Curse
  keeps both halves of every score, so this is a read of the game's own data rather than a guess.
- **🧭 Identify area** tells you which of the sixteen levels you are standing on, by matching the
  level data the game has loaded. Use it after the position lock; every level is 16×16, so the
  coordinates alone cannot tell you.
- Do position work while **exploring**, never mid-combat, and take a step afterwards to redraw.
