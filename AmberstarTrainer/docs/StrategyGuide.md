# Amberstar Strategy Guide

## Game Overview

**Amberstar** is a 1992 role-playing game by **Thalion Software**, originally released for the Atari ST and later ported to DOS as the **IBM AT version 1.34**. You lead a party of up to six adventurers across Lyramion, seeking the thirteen fragments of the Amberstar and the means to defeat the sorcerer **Marmion**.

Exploration uses grid-based movement, while encounters use turn-based combat. Progress depends as much on conversation, clues, and useful items as it does on fighting: inspect places carefully, collect vocabulary keywords, and revisit NPCs when you learn something new.

## Controls

### Movement and the Mouse

| Input | Result |
|---|---|
| Numeric keypad `7` | Move northwest |
| Numeric keypad `8` | Move north |
| Numeric keypad `9` | Move northeast |
| Numeric keypad `4` | Move west |
| Numeric keypad `6` | Move east |
| Numeric keypad `1` | Move southwest |
| Numeric keypad `2` | Move south |
| Numeric keypad `3` | Move southeast |
| Mouse | Click interface icons and interact with the world |

Use the corresponding movement icons if you prefer the mouse. Movement is grid-based, so diagonal movement is often useful for covering open terrain efficiently.

### Action Icons

| Icon | Use |
|---|---|
| **Eye** | Look at or examine a person, object, sign, or location. |
| **Hand** | Use, activate, take, or manipulate an item or world object. |
| **Zzz** | Rest or advance time. |
| **Auto-map** | View the current area's map. |
| **Attack** | Enter or confirm combat actions. |

Keyboard shortcuts also cover movement and other common actions; use the icons when a shortcut is unclear.

### Combat, Conversation, and Time

- **Combat** is turn-based. Choose an action for each character, then execute the round with the single action icon press. Plan the whole round before committing it.
- **Conversations** use a keyword vocabulary. Click known keywords to ask an NPC about them. New dialogue, signs, and notices can add important words, so speak to everyone and revisit people after learning clues.
- **Time** passes through the day/night cycle and changes visibility. Use the **Zzz** icon deliberately when an event requires a particular time, such as Gwendoline's midday appearance in the Crypt.

## Character Creation and Development

### Races

Choose from seven races. Each has different attribute limits, so race matters most when planning a character's long-term role.

| Race | General strengths |
|---|---|
| Human | Flexible, broadly capable adventurer. |
| Elf | Naturally suited to magical and agile roles. |
| Dwarf | Durable front-line character. |
| Gnome | Useful for technical and magical specializations. |
| Halfling | Nimble, stealth-oriented adventurer. |
| Half-Elf | A flexible blend of human and elven traits. |
| Half-Orc | Strong physical combatant. |

### Classes

| Class | Primary role |
|---|---|

| Paladin | Armoured fighter with supportive abilities. |
| Ranger | Outdoor and exploration-focused combatant. |
| Thief | Locks, traps, searching, and utility. |

| White Mage | Healing and recovery magic. |

### Attributes and Skills

Every character has nine core attributes:

- **Strength**
- **Intelligence**
- **Dexterity**
- **Speed**
- **Constitution**
- **Charisma**
- **Luck**
- **Anti-Magic**
- **Age**

The ten practical skills are **Attack**, **Parry**, **Swim**, **Listen**, **Find Traps**, **Disarm Traps**, **Pick Locks**, **Search**, **Read Magic Scrolls**, and **Use Magic**.

For a balanced first party, cover four essentials: a durable fighter, a thief, a healer, and an offensive or utility mage. Raise the thief's **Pick Locks** toward **95%** before spreading points widely; this prevents many avoidable dead ends and resource losses.

### Magic and Promotion

- **White magic** focuses on healing and recovery.
- **Grey magic** provides utility, buffs, and protective effects.
- **Black magic** provides offensive power.

Characters improve through guild promotion. Visit the appropriate guild, pay the required gold, and promote whenever eligible. Promotions grant skill points and additional HP/SP, so do not postpone them merely to hoard money.

## How to Play

### Early Game Strategy

1. Begin in **Twinlake Graveyard** and meet **Gwendoline** in the Crypt at **midday**.
2. Buy horses from **Twinlake Stables** for **150 gold per character** when possible. Horses greatly improve overland travel and cross rivers, though they cannot enter forests.
3. Recruit a capable core party:
   - **Drobonir**, Warrior — Gemstone Inn
   - **Trasric**, Black Wizard — Crystal Jewel Inn
   - **Silk**, Thief — Twinlake Dragon Inn
   - **Grylan**, Paladin — Crystal
   - **Satine**, Grey Wizard — Grey Wizard Tower
   - **Crag**, White Wizard — White Wizard Tower
4. Build early experience on **bridge trolls** and **sewer rats**. Keep enough healing supplies to return safely rather than pushing too far on a single excursion.
5. Complete the **Twinlake Sewers** and rescue Felix the cat. This teaches the Animal language and leads to recruiting **Spike the Dog**.

### Mid-Game Priorities

- Search **Jonathan's House**, the **Dragon Inn Cellars**, and the **Jewel Inn Cellars** thoroughly.
- Clear the **White**, **Grey**, and **Black Wizard Towers**. The towers provide wizard recruits and the passwords needed for guild-related progress.
- Destroy the Dragon's egg on **Dragon Keep**, then obtain the **Eagle flute** from Illien. The Eagle is the most flexible travel method.
- Retrieve the **Blue Disc** from **Pharaoh's Tomb** for rapid land and shallow-sea travel.
- Visit **Swampstation** for the **Map Locator**.

### Late-Game Fragment Hunt

The thirteen Amberstar pieces are tied to the following major quests and places. Do not assume a location is complete after one visit: inspect containers, speak to every resident, and follow the clues that each quest produces.

1. **Firlas's bone quest**
2. **Crystal Paladin chest**
3. **Dreblin's Cellar**
4. **Frog Pond**
5. **Jonathan's Lost Island**
6. **Dragon Keep**
7. **Tower of Riddles**, through Kelvin's harp
8. **Old Ranger Guild**
9. **Dwarf Mine**, including the Dwarven Crown
10. **Pharaoh's Tomb**
11. **Temple of Sansri**
12. **Cave of Manyeye**, through Melgrim
13. **Marmion**, in the final confrontation

Do the most dangerous dungeons—especially Pharaoh's Tomb and the Whirlpool cave—only after the party has reliable healing, strong equipment, and enough Anti-Magic support.

## How to Win

The final objective is to collect all thirteen pieces, assemble the Amberstar at the **Altar in the Forest of Unity**, gain access to the **Fortress of Godsbane**, and defeat Marmion.

The assembly sequence refers to these named pieces and figures:

| Order | Piece or figure | Source / note |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Mork | Monk Guild area |
| 2 | Bralkur | Mentioned in game lore |
| 3 | Sobek | Pharaoh's Tomb |
| 4 | Gala | Pharaoh's Tomb |
| 5 | Sansri | Temple of Sansri |
| 6 | Nut | Pharaoh's Tomb |
| 7 | Lord Tarbos | Game lore |
| 8 | Geb | Pharaoh's Tomb |
| 9 | Bala | Pharaoh's Tomb |
| 10 | Harachte | Pharaoh's Tomb |
| 11 | Marmion | Final boss, Fortress of Godsbane |
| 12 | Talmit | Final piece at the Forest of Unity altar |

Follow the altar's required ordering rather than trying to force progress elsewhere. Once the Amberstar is assembled, enter the Fortress of Godsbane prepared for the final battle and defeat Marmion to complete the game.

## Tips and Tricks

- **Save often.** The game has only one save slot, so save before major dungeons, difficult conversations, and any irreversible-looking choice.
- **Carry the Rod of Resurrection.** A recovery tool is much more valuable than spare treasure when a difficult area goes wrong.
- **Open battles with Anti-Magic.** It reduces the danger from hostile spellcasters and should be a standard early-round priority.
- **Maintain buffs.** Use **Armour Protection** and **Weapon Power** before or at the start of difficult fights.
- **Shop in Illien.** Its herbs are cheap, making it an excellent place to stock curatives for ailments.
- **Rest outside towns.** When safe, this avoids repeated inn costs.
- **Sell excess equipment.** Crystal's shop is open around the clock, making it a reliable place to convert unneeded loot into promotion money.
- **Use the Crystal Ball in forests.** It helps maintain direction where normal overland navigation is less clear.
- **Treat keywords as quest items.** Learn every word available from conversations, notices, and signs, then use new words with relevant NPCs.
- **Prepare before entering Pharaoh's Tomb or the Whirlpool cave.** Bring healing, anti-magic measures, supplies, and a well-promoted party.
- **Train Pick Locks early.** Reaching 95% on the thief's lockpicking skill is one of the best early investments.
- **Farm bridge trolls when needed.** They are efficient early experience sources once the party can defeat them consistently.

## Travel Methods

| Method | Cost / source | Capability | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Walking | Free | Goes anywhere on foot, including forests. | Slow and cannot cross water. |
| Horse | 150 gold per character | Crosses rivers and speeds overland travel. | Cannot enter forests. |
| Raft | Found free | Crosses shallow water. | Cannot use deep water. |
| Boat | 5,000 gold | Travels on deep water. | Expensive. |
| Blue Disc | Pharaoh's Tomb | Rapid land and shallow-sea travel. | Does not replace all transport needs. |
| Eagle | Illien quest | Flies almost everywhere. | Requires completing the Dragon Keep quest. |
| Windgates | Wind Necklace from Temple of Sansri | Fast travel through the Windgate network. | Requires the necklace and access to gates. |

A practical progression is **walking → horses/raft → boat or Blue Disc → Eagle and Windgates**. Keep walking available for forest travel even after acquiring better transport.
